# PIXIE funding strategy

## What we are raising for

PIXIE should be funded as a new creator medium / cultural intelligence workstation, not as a generic social scheduler and not as a DJ app with AI attached.

The near-term proof is a vertical slice that demonstrates:

`SIGNAL → CONTEXT → VERIFY → CLAIMS → NARRATIVE → CULTURAL MEMORY → MUSIC → CONVERSATION → VOICE → PUBLISH → ARCHIVE`

The first outside capital should de-risk the Story Engine, creator research, provenance layer, accessible workstation UI, and a small set of real-world adapters. Larger platform expansion comes after the vertical slice has evidence of creator demand.

## Capital structure to pursue

### 1. Non-dilutive arts / cultural capital
Best for the cultural experiment, creator research, public-facing work, archives, and new forms of expression.

Potential targets:
- Creative Capital — individual-artist project funding and professional support; its 2026 award explicitly includes technology, multidisciplinary, and socially engaged forms. Apply through the artist/project framing where eligible.
- National Endowment for the Arts — relevant to a nonprofit or eligible organizational partner, especially Visual & Media Arts, Music, Presenting & Multidisciplinary Works, or Design. Current Grants for Arts Projects rules exclude individuals, fiscal sponsors, and commercial for-profit enterprises, so PIXIE would need an eligible applicant/structure and the project must fit the guidelines.
- Mellon Foundation — approach through an eligible cultural institution, archive, collective, or institutional partner. Mellon explicitly supports institutions, collectives, initiatives, and new forms of expression in arts and culture.
- Sundance Institute — relevant if PIXIE develops a specific interactive/new-media artwork or artist-led narrative project rather than simply seeking product-development money.

### 2. Non-dilutive technology R&D

NSF America's Seed Fund is worth exploring only if PIXIE can articulate a genuine technical R&D problem rather than ordinary software development. NSF requires an innovative technology, technical risk, R&D objectives, market opportunity, and a qualified U.S. small-business team. Project Pitches are the required first step; current NSF materials describe up to $305K Phase I and up to $1.25M Phase II, with additional supplements potentially available.

A plausible NSF framing would be: a provenance-aware narrative intelligence system that can model, navigate, and recombine multimodal cultural evidence while preserving distinctions between source material, claims, interpretation, and authored narrative. Do not pitch the existing UI as the technical innovation by itself.

### 3. Mission-aligned venture / strategic capital

Once the vertical slice has creator evidence, approach investors who understand creator tools, media technology, music technology, or new media.

Potential conversations:
- Runway Fund — explicitly focused on AI, media, and new media/content; announced a 2026 fund with typical pre-seed/seed checks up to $500K.
- a16z Cultural Leadership Fund / creator-stack ecosystem — relevant because its published creator-stack thesis covers tools for independent artists, musicians, writers, production, publishing, distribution, and community.
- Mindset MusicTech — early-stage investor focused on Music, Sound, and Audio technology, especially creator/workflow software.
- Worklife — creator-oriented venture platform with a network around creative work and independent builders.
- Other media/entertainment and creator-tool seed investors should be approached after the demo and pilot generate evidence; use warm introductions wherever possible.

## Funding sequence

### Stage A — prove the medium
Target: enough capital to finish the vertical slice, conduct creator research, and produce a credible demo.

Use of funds:
- Story Engine / persistence
- product design + accessibility
- creator research and pilot cohort
- provenance / evidence model
- demo production
- legal/IP/data-rights review

### Stage B — prove repeat use
Target: a small seed round or larger grant stack after pilot evidence.

Use of funds:
- research/context adapters
- music metadata + local-library intelligence
- conversation/publishing adapters
- archive and story revision
- native shell work
- small core engineering/product team

### Stage C — build the platform
Only after product evidence supports it:
- AT Protocol identity/social layer
- external music/audio services
- live/streaming adapters
- advanced AI research/context tooling
- broader iOS/iPadOS productization

## What funders should see

The funder demo should answer one question in five minutes:

> What happens when a creator notices something in the world and PIXIE helps them turn that signal into a researched, contextualized, musical, conversational, authored story without taking the author's voice away?

The demo should use a real creator workflow and show provenance at every transition.

## Important positioning

Avoid:
- “AI social media scheduler”
- “AI DJ assistant”
- “content automation platform”
- “another creator dashboard”

Prefer:
- “cultural intelligence workstation”
- “authoring and playback system for cultural stories”
- “a creator medium where research, narrative, music, conversation, and memory can be authored together”

## Partner types to recruit

Before a large raise, recruit evidence from:
- 5–10 DJs / selectors
- 5 independent musicians/producers
- 3–5 visual artists / writers / culture workers
- 1–2 archives, museums, universities, or cultural organizations
- 1 accessibility/product-design advisor
- 1 music-rights / licensing advisor
- 1 experienced startup/product engineering advisor

The goal is not a large beta. The goal is strong qualitative evidence and a few repeat workflows that demonstrate why PIXIE is different.
